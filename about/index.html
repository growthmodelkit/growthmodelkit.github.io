Who I am

I have worked in five IT companies, in very different roles: software development, data analysis, machine learning. In mature companies and in fast-growing startups, I kept seeing the same thing: big projects that took months of a team's work and changed nothing for the business. The expectations were big, but nobody had checked them against the numbers.

That is what led me to product and growth analytics, and to growth models. It is the role where I found I can influence a company the most: help a team see what a piece of work can bring before they start it.

What I can do for you

I build a growth model of your product: a short description of how the product grows, and an interactive calculator on your real data, like the tools on this site but made for you. Where the data cannot prove a link yet, the model says so, and you can test it.

From your side: access to the data, and a few meetings with the team to learn how the product works, what you measure and why.

What a model gives you

  • Product metrics in money. A model links the numbers a team works on, such as a sign-up funnel or feature adoption, to long-term revenue. Then any work can be compared by what it brings.
  • Assumptions you can test. Some links in a model are guesses: nobody has tested them yet. A model writes them down, so you know what to test first, and you update the model when you learn something new.
  • A common language. With a model, the team talks about the same numbers and the same assumptions, not about whose view is stronger. It also helps you walk your boss or investors through a decision: what you expect from it, and why.
